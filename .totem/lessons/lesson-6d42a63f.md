## Lesson — The strict eligibility schema checks keys, not consistency

**Tags:** schema, authoring-ledger, mixed-versions, spine
**Scope:** packages/core/src/spine/authored-rule.ts

`StructEligResultSchema` in core is strict since mmnto-ai/totem#2996: a reader at that version fails on a result key it does not know, where the earlier schema stripped the key without a word. The strictness broke nothing on disk. All 34 rows of this repository's authoring ledger carried exactly the three keys `decidable`, `basis` and `judgedBy` at the merge head and all passed, measured by the seat and again by the bring-forward leg. What it changes is the mixed-version gap, and in one direction only: a reader older than 2.14.0 still strips the optional `scopeConjunct` silently, and a writer older than 2.14.0 re-derives a tagged rule to the three-key result, reads it as `revised` and appends a three-key row that the newer version reads as `revised` again. So a re-pinned ledger is read and written at 2.14.0 or later. Strict here means closed key sets, not cross-field consistency: the bring-forward leg parsed a hand-written `decidable: true` beside an unsatisfied conjunct, and a conjunct on an untagged class, and both passed. The check `evaluateStructuralEligibility` never produces either shape, and the cert path re-derives every entry instead of trusting its recorded row, so the cross-field refine was declined and the gap is stated in the field's comment. A contract sentence saying an unsatisfied conjunct makes the result not decidable is a statement about the check, not about what the reader accepts.
