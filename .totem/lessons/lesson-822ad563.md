## Lesson — A re-worded status sentence is checked against its primary

**Tags:** docs, freeze, ruling, falsification-leg, trap, s6, prose-derivation

**Applies-to:** presentation

A status sentence re-worded under a ruling is checked clause by clause against the ruling's text and the predicate it describes, never against the shape of the sentence it replaces. In S6 step 8 (mmnto-ai/totem#3066) the first cut said the corpus was held read-only until a replacement route passed held-out validation, kept "the compiled corpus remains fully enforced" beside a README line saying 4 of 264 active rules block, blamed a failing CI lint step on demoted legacy rules that cannot fail it, and gave the parked compile as the cause of a demotion that the engine predicate performs on its own. Falsification read 1 refuted each at its source: the lift block of record says gate (5) produced no held-out evidence; the linter fails only when the blocking-set filter is non-empty; the regex class is demoted by the engine predicate regardless of the freeze. Read 2 then removed a one-machine firing metric from a comment's reasons because the ruling declined that basis. The cure that held: for every clause that states what a system does or why, name the primary (the ruling's line, the predicate in code) and quote or derive from it; a sentence that reads well against its neighbour is not evidence.
